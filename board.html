Welcome · Ice breaker · 9:15 AM · 10 min

Number Line-Up

Ten participants each hold one A4 card, numbered 1 to 10. A number appears on screen. The people holding its cards run up and line up in the right order.

Round 1
1023
0:30
When your line is ready, each person says

“Hi, I'm Alia, No. 10.”

“Hi, I'm Ravi, No. 2.”

“Hi, I'm Siti, No. 3.”

All together: “And we are number set 1023!”

How to play
  1. Give ten people one A4 card each, numbered 1 to 10. Everyone else is the cheering crowd.
  2. Press New number. Each number is built only from cards 1 to 10, with no card used twice. 1023 is 10 + 2 + 3.
  3. Card holders in the number run up and stand left to right in order. 30 seconds.
  4. Each person introduces themselves with their card number, then the group calls out its number set.
  5. Swap cards between rounds so new people play. Finish with All 10.
The point of the game

We are all data, from the minute we walk into the office

This isn't just an ice breaker. Each card on its own is just a number. In the right order, the same cards become a meaningful set: 1023 and 2310 use the same cards and mean completely different things.

Every one of us comes from a different background, but data matters to all of us. You may look simple, but you are carrying data right now: you badged in at a time, you parked somewhere, you bought a coffee, you opened your email. That is all data, and it means something once it is put in order.

Module 1 · Lego game · 15 min · groups of 5

The Data Brick Challenge

Build with everything, then cut it down to what the question needs. That is what cleaning and filtering data feels like.

1What is data?

Facts we record about what happens: every sale, every timesheet, every complaint, every click. On its own it is just bricks in a box.

2Why learn dashboards?

Because nobody has time to read a pile of raw data. A dashboard sorts the bricks so the answer is visible in seconds.

3Why do reporting dashboards matter?

Managers decide every day. A trusted dashboard means they decide on facts, everyone sees the same numbers, and problems show up early.

The scenario

Kopi Kita's data pile

Kak Mira owns Kopi Kita, a café with three outlets. Every day her systems collect data: cups sold, receipts, staff hours, stock, complaints, social media likes. It all lands in one big pile, and nobody has sorted it.

Each colour of brick is a different kind of data. Your group's tower is everything Kopi Kita collected this month. Kak Mira doesn't need all of it. She needs one answer.

    Module 1 · 9:53 AM · 9 min

    The Big Why

    Start from a decision, not from the data. Everyone posts on one shared Miro board.

    Three sticky notes each
    1. Yellow sticky"One decision I make every month is…"
    2. Blue sticky"The number that would change that decision is…"
    3. Green sticky"My three KPIs are…"

    Example: "I decide how many temp staff to hire each month. The number that changes it is orders per staff hour. My KPIs: orders per hour, overtime hours, late deliveries."
