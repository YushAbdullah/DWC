Welcome · 9:08 AM

Ground rules

Eight rules for a hands-on morning. We agree them together before we start building.

  1. Phones on silent, laptops openWe build today, not just watch. Keep your laptop charged and Power BI open.
  2. Try first, then askStuck for two minutes? Raise your hand or ask your neighbour. Nobody gets left behind.
  3. No silly questionsIf you are wondering about it, someone else in the room is too.
  4. Synthetic data onlyUse the practice files today. Don't upload real company or personal data (PDPA).
  5. Watch the timerEvery activity has a timer on screen. When it ends, we come back together.
  6. Park it on MiroOff-topic or big questions go on the Parking Lot. We answer them before we close.
  7. Challenge the data, not the personEvery view is welcome. Ask what the numbers show and where they came from.
  8. Help your neighbourExplaining a step to someone else is the fastest way to learn it yourself.

Module 1 · Case study · 15 min · teams of 4

The December staffing call

Why is data so important, and why don't we build the dashboard straight away? Teams find out with a bag of Lego bricks.

The scenario

Aventra Mart has one extra staff member for December

Aventra Mart runs three convenience stores in the Klang Valley: Store A in Shah Alam, Store B in Petaling Jaya and Store C in Cheras. December is the busiest month of the year.

Head office can fund one extra full-time staff member, for one store only. The regional manager, Puan Aina, needs an answer by lunch.

You are the analytics team. Last month's data has just arrived from three store systems, and it came as a bag of bricks.

The manager's question (revealed after Round 1)Which store should get the extra staff member in December?
The data key

Every brick is a record

  • Red brick = 10 sales transactions
  • Blue brick = 10 staff hours worked
  • Yellow brick = 1 customer complaint
  • White brick = a record with no store label (unknown)
  • Two bricks stuck together = the same record entered twice (duplicate)
  • Tiny 1×1 pieces = noise: test entries and system logs

Four rounds

1
Build it now3 min

Hand each team the three store bags. Say only: "Head office wants a dashboard. Build one." Don't give the question.

Teams build something tall and colourful. It looks like a dashboard, but nobody asked what decision it supports.

3:00
2
Ask, sort and clean5 min

Reveal the question card. Ask: "Does your build answer it?" Then teams agree the KPI, sort by store and colour, and clean.

  • Pull apart stuck pairs and keep one (duplicates)
  • Set white bricks aside (unknown store)
  • Drop the 1×1 pieces (noise)
  • Count red, blue and yellow for each store
5:00
3
Build the story4 min

One tower pair per store: red tower for sales next to a blue tower for staff hours, with yellow complaints on top.

Write one sentence for Puan Aina: the store, the number, the reason.

4:00
4
Debrief3 min
  • What did you build in Round 1? Did it answer the question?
  • What changed once you had the question first?
  • Which bricks did you remove, and why?
  • Which store looked biggest before you cleaned the data?

Module 1 · 9:53 AM · 9 min

The Big Why

Start from a decision, not from the data. Everyone posts on one shared Miro board.

Three sticky notes each
  1. Yellow sticky"One decision I make every month is…"
  2. Blue sticky"The number that would change that decision is…"
  3. Green sticky"My three KPIs are…"

Example: "I decide how many temp staff to hire each month. The number that changes it is orders per staff hour. My KPIs: orders per hour, overtime hours, late deliveries."
